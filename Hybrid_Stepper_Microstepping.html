Air gap, unrolled

Poles A+, B+, A−, B− sit 45° apart, which is 6.25 tooth pitches. Each pole's teeth are offset a quarter pitch from the previous pole's, so moving the magnetic pull from one pole to the next shifts the rotor 7.2° / 4 = 1.8°. Microstepping splits the pull between two poles to park the rotor in between.

Why 1.8° per full step

The rotor has 50 teeth on a 7.2° pitch. Energizing poles in the order A+, B+, A−, B− moves the attraction a quarter pitch each time: 360° / (4 × 50) = 1.8°, or 200 full steps per revolution.

What microstepping does

The driver sets IA = I cos φ and IB = I sin φ. Torque equilibrium sits at electrical angle φ, so the shaft angle is φ / 50. At 1/8 that gives 1,600 positions per revolution, 0.225° each.

Limits in a real motor

Smaller current jumps excite far less ringing near the resonant step rate. Absolute accuracy is another matter: detent torque and non-sinusoidal torque curves make microstep positions uneven, and the holding torque gained per microstep scales with sin(90°/n).
