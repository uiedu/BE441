Phasors → sum x(t)

Each sine is a rotating arrow of length A, spinning at f turns per second, starting at angle φ. Chained tip to tail, the height of the last tip is the sum of all enabled sines, traced on the left.

Individual sine waves

A·sin(2πft + φ) for each component, drawn on the same amplitude scale.

Spectrum

The same signal in the frequency domain: one line per sine, its height the amplitude and, below, its phase.

Things to try

Gibbs overshootPick Square and push Harmonics to 21. The corners sharpen, but the overshoot at each edge stays near 9%.
Phase mattersPress Scramble phases on a square wave. The amplitude spectrum is unchanged, yet the waveform looks nothing like a square.
BeatsTwo sines 0.5 Hz apart swell and fade every 2 s. Slide one frequency to change the beat rate.
Brain rhythmsPick EEG α alpha. Its sines sit within about 1 Hz of each other, so the trace pulses in bursts. Shaded bands on the spectrum mark δ, θ, α, β and γ.
Lub-dub by earPick Heart sounds, play the original recording, then the sine sum. Drop the Sines slider to 12 and play again: the beat turns into a muddy hum because each short click needs many neighbouring lines.
Not periodic?Use frequencies that are not integer multiples (try EMG). The sum never repeats exactly within the window.
